Whitney Extension Problem

In the broadest terms, Whitney problems concern making optimal decisions from limited information. A typical problem in this field asks for efficient and universal ways to fit a function to unstructured data while minimizing some energies. I am particularly interested in extension problem with constraints on the codomain. Here is an expository article with an overview of Whitney's problems. Matching Networks and Pointclouds

The Gromov-Wasserstein (GW) distance is a powerful tool from optimal transport for comparing datasets across different metric spaces, widely used in network analysis and geometry processing. However, GW is sensitive to outliers and cannot handle partial matching. We study a relaxed version of GW and analyze its geometric properties. Building on this, we introduce a new family of robust distances that overcome GW's limitations and offer improved robustness to noise and incomplete data. \(L_1\) localization and sparsity estimation

A problem posed by H. Feichtinger (and subsequently by C. Heil and D. Larson) asks whether an \(L_1\) version of Mercer's Theorem will hold. One can formulate the finite-dimensional variant of the problem in terms of an optimal matrix factorization problem in \(\ell_1\). In a recent paper with R. Balan, we showed that the answer is no in general, but we also provide interesting positive cases with connection to classical subjects such nuclear and 2-summing operators.
